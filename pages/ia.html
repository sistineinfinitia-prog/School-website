Internal Assessment (Computational Solution)

Official coursework specification for the redesigned IB Computer Science syllabus (First Assessment May 2027). A 35-hour individual engineering project marked out of 30 marks, weighted at 30% for Standard Level and 20% for Higher Level.

Showing 5 Assessment Criteria • 30 Marks Total • Redesigned 2027 Syllabus Framework
2027 Syllabus Paradigm Shift: No External Client Required Official Syllabus Update

A major update in the redesigned IB Computer Science syllabus is that students are NO LONGER required to have a real individual, external third-party client, or formal business contract. You do not need formal client interview transcripts, business sign-off letters, or workplace advisors.

Instead, the IA is defined as an authentic computational solution to an identified problem. The core requirement is that you identify a real-world problem or intellectual challenge that demands computational thinking, algorithmic complexity, structured data modeling, and empirical verification. The "user" can be a user group, school club, academic peer cohort, simulated community, or even an optimization tool for personal scientific exploration.

The Redesigned 2027 Internal Assessment Architecture

Streamlined 30-mark framework replacing the legacy 34-mark specification for first assessment in May 2027.

The Internal Assessment (IA) for the redesigned International Baccalaureate Diploma Programme (IB DP) Computer Science course is an individual engineering project that challenges students to solve a genuine, authentic problem through computational thinking and software development.

30 Total Marks Available Across 5 Criteria (A–E)
30% / 20% Course Weighting 30% SL • 20% HL (Comp. 03)
~35 hrs Instruction & Dev Dedicated Engineering Time

02

The Client Requirement Dispelled: Authentic Problem Solving

Official clarification on why commercial third-party client interviews are no longer mandatory.

2027 Syllabus Shift: No External Client Required Official Syllabus Update

Under previous guides, students often struggled to find authentic commercial clients, resulting in scripted interviews. The 2027 curriculum removes the external client mandate.

Examiners now evaluate whether candidates address a genuine computational problem that requires algorithmic complexity, structured data manipulation, and empirical verification. The user can be a student club, teacher cohort, academic peers, simulated user group, or an optimization tool for personal scientific research.

Approved Computational Problem Domains

IB examiners strongly reward authentic problems that demand algorithmic automation and structured models. Five premier domains include:

DOM-01

School & Community Operational Tools

Automated timetable engines, library asset management, tournament match scheduling with room/clash conflict resolution.

Algorithmic Scheduling • SQLite CRUD
DOM-02

Scientific & Mathematical Simulators

Orbital dynamics simulators, epidemiological spread calculators, discrete-event queue models, cellular automata.

Numerical Precision • State Modeling
DOM-03

Algorithmic & Educational Platforms

Sorting and graph pathfinding visualizers (Dijkstra, A*), adaptive flashcard engines utilizing spaced-repetition heuristics (SuperMemo SM-2).

Data Structures • Heuristics
DOM-04

Data Analytics & Relational Utilities

Relational management dashboards with custom filtering, data sanitization pipelines, financial budgeting, and automated PDF report compilation.

SQL Normalized Schema • Integrity Checks
DOM-05

Algorithmic Games & Physics Engines

Strategy games (Chess, Connect Four, Othello) powered by Minimax with alpha-beta pruning, or custom arcade 2D collision physics engines.

Game Loops • Collision Hitboxes • AI Trees
03

The Golden Chain of Consistency (Examiner Principle)

The single most critical factor in achieving Grade 7 (26–30 marks) in the redesigned IA.

According to IB Chief Examiner reports and moderation guidelines, high-scoring projects maintain an unbroken chain of consistency across all five criteria. Every success criterion established in Criterion A must be planned in B, architected in C, empirically verified in D, and appraised in E:

CRITERION A

Specify Criteria

Formulate 5–8 quantitative, testable success criteria [SC-01..SC-07].

→
CRITERION B

Decompose Modules

Break requirements into sprints with a chronological record of tasks.

→
CRITERION C

Model & Test Plan

Design UML, database schemas, and create a 3-tier test plan for SC-01..07.

→
CRITERION D

Code & Test Proof

Implement algorithms and capture screenshot proof for every single planned test.

→
CRITERION E

Appraise & Extend

Audit each SC-01..07 status with user feedback and technical extensions.

The Golden Rule of Traceability

If an examiner finds a feature evaluated in E that was never tested in D, or a test in D that was never specified in A, your project will be penalized for fragmented planning. Tag every criterion with an explicit ID (e.g. SC-01) and preserve it verbatim throughout your entire document.

04

Technical Stack, Languages & Data Persistence

Mandatory programming standards, object-oriented design, and database persistence requirements.

Recommended Language Stack

The IB does not mandate a specific language, but the chosen stack must support OOP classes, modular separation, and non-trivial algorithms.

  • Python 3.x (Primary Recommendation): Clean syntax, robust standard library, rapid prototyping, and extensive packages.
  • GUI Frameworks: PyQt6 or CustomTkinter for modern responsive interfaces; standard Tkinter is acceptable.
  • Java / C#: Fully accepted if students possess advanced fluency in strongly typed object-oriented software engineering.

Data Persistence Mandate

RAM-only variables will severely cap Criterion D marks. Closing the program must NOT reset state. Students must implement structured disk persistence:

SQLite 3 (Best Practice) Normalized relational tables, primary/foreign keys, and parameterized SQL queries.
Structured JSON / CSV Hierarchical JSON schemas with defensive file error handling and validation logic.
05

Academic Integrity & Generative AI Regulations

Official IB DP policy regarding LLMs, GitHub Copilot, third-party libraries, and authorship.

Emirates International School Meadows enforces the official IB Academic Integrity Policy across all coursework submissions. Large Language Models (LLMs) and code assistants may only be used within the strict boundaries below:

PERMITTED

Original Code Authorship

All core algorithmic logic, data processing, and custom classes must be designed, written, and debugged personally by the candidate.

MANDATORY CITATION

AI Brainstorming & Debugging Declaration

If generative AI was used for concept brainstorming or syntax error troubleshooting, you must document the tool, date, prompts, and output in an appendix.

PERMITTED

Open-Source Libraries

Standard libraries (e.g. pygame, sqlite3, requests, matplotlib) are fully allowed, provided they are cited in Criterion D's library declaration table.

STRICTLY PROHIBITED

AI Code Generation of Core Logic

Submitting AI-generated algorithms, complete modules, or documentation as your own constitutes academic misconduct and leads to zero marks.

06

Coursework Assembly & Final Submission Checklist

Mandatory pre-submission verification steps prior to internal marking and IBO moderation upload.

01

Single Master PDF Document

The entire written coursework covering Criteria A through E must be compiled into a single PDF document for official upload.

02

Strict Word Count Management

Maintain the recommended limit of approximately 2,000 to 3,000 words. Code listings, UML diagrams, test execution tables, and bibliographies are excluded.

03

Numbered Success Criteria Traceability

Confirm that every success criterion defined in Criterion A (SC-01..SC-07) is referenced verbatim across Criteria C, D, and E.

04

Empirical Screenshot Evidence

Every planned test row in Criterion D must include an accompanying screenshot proving actual execution with normal, boundary, and abnormal inputs.

05

Strict Candidate Anonymity

Remove all student names, teacher names, and school names from the headers, footers, and body text. Retain only your IB candidate session code.

06

Video Demonstration (MP4)

Record a concise video demonstration (maximum 5 minutes) showing the working solution executing core functionality and fulfilling success criteria.
